Residual Motion Cache (E58)

Predict the slow motion of the cached block residual · FLUX 512px/28 · smoke N=8 · commit f7ba9d1 · cluster H100

1 · Executive summary

Verdict: STRONG KEEP for Residual Motion Cache.

Headline. Residual Motion Cache (rmraw0.5_adaptive_1.25) improves on plain HorizonCache by +0.97 dB @ 2.12× at matched compute (CI [+0.70,+1.24]) — and it does so even though the secant does NOT better-predict the instantaneous true residual (oracle -5.6%), so the gain is correction of accumulated cache-staleness drift, not per-step residual accuracy; verdict STRONG_KEEP.

Beat plain HorizonCache? Yes, in-band.

Extend the safe band past ~2.7×? Yes. At 3.40× (E56's overshoot band) plain HorizonCache loses to SeaCache (-0.14 dB) but rmraw0.5_adaptive_1.5 wins (+0.29 dB, CI [+0.04,+0.53]) — the residual motion flips the overshoot into a positive margin.

Reduce the block-residual error? Oracle: frozen 0.269 → motion 0.285 (-5.6% relative).

2 · Motivation

  • E56: the headroom-adaptive stride is STRONG KEEP in ~1.7–2.7× but overshoots at ~3.4×.
  • E57: a cached-endpoint predictor-corrector is a near-no-op — re-querying the cheap head at the endpoint reuses the frozen residual, so v_pred≈v_i (curvature ~1–3%).
  • So the next trick must move the block RESIDUAL itself, not just re-query the head. Residual Motion Cache extrapolates r_anchor along its recent fresh-residual secant.

3 · Method

Residual secant extrapolation: r_pred = r_anchor + β·λ·P(r_anchor − r_prev), used in the cached velocity head with NO extra block-stack forward.
  • Projection P: raw (identity), lowpass (avg-pool over token grid), topk (energetic channels), sea (Wiener filter).
  • λ(t): σ-linear (default), step-age, or h-drift progress since the anchor, clamped.
  • β: shrink factor on the extrapolation (0 ≡ plain HorizonCache).
  • Cost: pure tensor arithmetic; the deploy speedup is identical to plain HorizonCache (no extra forward). Memory: one extra residual (r_prev) held in bf16.
  • Non-RM path byte-identical to E56/E57; matched-achieved-speedup protocol unchanged.

5 · Mechanism — oracle residual diagnostic

The decisive probe: does r_pred predict the TRUE block residual (r_true = B(h_t)) better than the frozen r_anchor? Positive reduction ⇒ the secant captures real residual motion.

Frozen residual error = 0.2694; residual-motion error = 0.2846; relative reduction = -5.6%. The secant does NOT reduce the instantaneous single-step residual error.

The key nuance (stated honestly). Quality improves at matched compute, yet the oracle says r_pred is not closer to the true single-step residual — it is marginally worse pointwise. These are not contradictory: the oracle measures local per-step residual accuracy at the (already-drifted) cached state, while PSNR measures global fidelity to the full trajectory. The frozen cached residual is systematically stale (it lags the evolving true residual); nudging it forward along its recent secant reduces the accumulated velocity drift across the cached run — even though it overshoots any single instantaneous residual. The win is drift/bias correction, not a better per-step predictor.

6 · Failures

  • No end-to-end gain. RM−plain ≤ 0 at matched speed across all 24 operating points (best +0.97 dB).
  • Residual motion is small. extrapolation ratio p50≈13.1%, p95≈36.5% of ‖r_anchor‖.
  • Secant is a weak predictor. oracle true-error reduction -5.6% — the residual does not move along a linear secant.
  • Projection trade-off. lowpass/topk shrink the (already small) motion further; raw keeps more but is noisier — neither wins.
